# Vespi: the value of decentralized micro relationships

A new infrastructure to recognize, connect and distribute the value of human interactions

*Research essay by Andrés Peña and Francisco Toro. It describes the direction behind Vespi beyond the Instawards sprint, not what the sprint delivers.*

## 1. The value we still don't recognize

Technology is quickly changing how we produce, work and relate to each other. Artificial intelligence, algorithms, robotics and new manufacturing systems are changing the traditional ways of creating value. In this setting one question matters more and more: what place will people have in the economies of the future?

An important part of the answer lies in something that has always been there and that we have rarely managed to recognize economically: the small relationships of collaboration, trust and contribution that people build every day.

Helping someone solve a problem, sharing knowledge, accompanying a process, giving a recommendation, making an introduction or working together on a task all produce value. Many of these actions stay invisible to traditional economic systems. They are seen as goodwill, informal help, volunteer work or simple personal exchanges. Their contribution can matter a lot to the people involved, yet it is rarely recorded, certified or tied to any chance of being rewarded.

This invisibility is an opportunity for decentralized economies.

Micro relationships are small units of social interaction in which two or more people coordinate an action, make a contribution and produce a result. They are small in scale, but they underlie much larger processes: trust, cooperation, learning, association and economic development.

The challenge is to recognize that a human interaction can produce real value, and that this value can be identified, certified, shared and eventually rewarded.

## 2. Decentralized micro relationships: an opportunity for Web3

Web3 proposes a new way to organize trust and exchange. In traditional models, much of the validation and distribution of value depends on central intermediaries. Decentralized infrastructure lets different participants record, verify and coordinate actions through shared rules.

There is, however, a wide gap between the technological promise of Web3 and people's everyday experience.

We often have decentralized technology and still use interaction models inherited from Web2. The tools may be new, while participation still depends on centralized platforms, intermediary organizations and user experiences that do little for participants' autonomy.

Distributing the technological infrastructure is only part of decentralization. It also requires changing how people meet, agree, collaborate and get recognized for their contributions.

This is where decentralized micro relationships matter.

Instead of trying to bring people into complex technological systems, we can start with something they already know: a concrete interaction with another person. A small action, easy to understand, with a result you can point to. If that action can be agreed on, carried out, certified and recognized, it becomes a basic unit of decentralized exchange.

This lets Web3 adoption start from human experience rather than from technological complexity.

Decentralization also has to happen in relationships, in people's ability to start agreements, recognize contributions and take part in distributing the value they produce.

## 3. Why Vespi needs to exist

Vespi addresses a concrete gap: the distance between the value people produce through their interactions and the ability of today's systems to recognize and reward it.

Its purpose is to build a simple infrastructure that turns certain social contributions into verifiable units of value.

Vespi Contributions lets two or more participants set the parameters and scope of an interaction in advance. Once the action is done, it can be certified with a verifiable record on a blockchain. The certificate records that the contribution happened and the conditions that were agreed.

From that record, the contribution can gain value within a system of recognition and reward defined by its participants or by the rules of the ecosystem.

What is new is a possibility that is still underdeveloped today: rewarding an action economically after it has been carried out, as long as its conditions were agreed beforehand and its completion can be verified.

This means the value of a contribution does not have to depend only on a formal hiring process, a traditional employment relationship or an organization acting as intermediary. People can make direct agreements, recognize each other's contributions and take part in ways of being rewarded.

Vespi does not aim to turn every human relationship into a commercial transaction. Its focus is narrower: the interactions that involve a recognizable contribution, an agreement between participants and a legitimate opportunity to create shared value.

A relationship that might once have stayed invisible can then leave evidence, build trust, create opportunities and take part in a distributed economy.

## 4. People's micro sustainability

Sustainability is usually analyzed at the level of large systems: countries, companies, industries, territories and organizations. There is also a basic dimension that starts with the individual: each person's ability to sustain their activities, develop their skills, build useful relationships and be recognized for what they contribute.

We can call this dimension micro sustainability.

Every day a person takes part in many collaborative relationships. Some bring direct economic benefits; others bring knowledge, contacts, trust, experience or new opportunities. Much of that value stays scattered, and the person cannot have it recognized or use it later.

Vespi wants to help these small contributions gain continuity and recognition.

By recording and certifying a contribution, a person can build evidence of their participation, make their skills visible and access ways of being rewarded. Many small recognized contributions can widen their economic opportunities and strengthen their place in networks of collaboration.

We are not claiming that every micro interaction will produce income, or that rewards will replace employment or traditional systems of economic protection. The proposal is to open an additional space where people can be recognized for contributions that today fall outside the usual ways of assigning value.

Micro sustainability grows when people do not depend on a single source of recognition or a single economic structure, and can take part in diverse networks where their skills and contributions are visible.

In this sense, Vespi wants human capital to be understood also as a living record of contributions, relationships and demonstrated skills, beyond credentials, degrees and work history.

## 5. The dimensions of Vespi

Vespi Contributions works across several dimensions that complement each other.

### Technological dimension: an infrastructure for interactions

Vespi proposes an infrastructure for interrelation that identifies who carried out an action, in what context, under what agreement and with what verifiable result. Blockchain technology backs the record and produces certificates that can be shared and recognized within an ecosystem.

Beyond storing information, it gives different participants shared references so they can coordinate and trust the contributions on record.

### Associative dimension: more capacity to collaborate

People associate because they need each other. Many of these relationships, though, have no clear way to recognize what each participant contributes. Collaboration often rests on goodwill, with no simple way to document its scope or share the benefits it creates.

Vespi seeks to strengthen these relationships by making direct agreements easier, cutting unnecessary intermediaries and letting participants set their own conditions for recognition and reward.

Help and solidarity keep their importance. What grows is the range of contributions that can be recognized, whether altruistic, collaborative, professional or economic.

### Economic dimension: bringing value where today there is invisibility

By letting contributions be recognized and eventually rewarded, Vespi widens the space of distributed economies. Small actions can connect with concrete economic opportunities and create new forms of exchange.

The expected result is an economy more open to people's skills, where value is produced both inside large organizations and in the relationships built between individuals and communities.

### Experimental and applied research dimension: learning from people

Vespi is also a platform for experimentation. It lets us study how people take part in decentralized interactions, how much effort it takes them to set up an agreement, what level of recognition they consider fair and what conditions make a collaboration repeat.

Understanding how effort, experience, trust and gratification relate is essential for designing better decentralized systems.

This knowledge will help identify which kinds of interactions can last over time, which create the most value for their participants and what conditions make Web3 easier to adopt for people outside the technology world.

## 6. Vespi as the social choreographer of decentralized relationships

One of Vespi's most important features can be understood through a metaphor: a social choreographer.

A good choreographer does not dictate every movement of the dancers. The work is to design the conditions in which people with different skills, experiences and rhythms can coordinate and reach a shared result.

There is no need to control each participant all the time. The choreographer sets shared references, makes the actions easy to understand and creates an environment where each person can act with autonomy.

Vespi seeks to play a similar role.

In a decentralized ecosystem, people need to meet, make agreements, understand their positions, make contributions and see how their actions relate to those of others. If every interaction requires complex instructions or a central authority to coordinate it, decentralization loses part of its meaning.

Vespi proposes a basic choreography of interaction: identify an opportunity to collaborate, agree on its conditions, carry out the action, certify that it was done and enable recognition of its value.

This sequence turns a spontaneous relationship into an experience people can understand and verify, without controlling how they behave.

The goal is to need fewer instructions and to increase people's capacity to coordinate on their own.

A good social choreography produces understanding, trust and autonomy, and it succeeds when participants can start new relationships, coordinate their actions and create collaborations without relying on whoever designed the tool.

## 7. How Vespi creates value

Vespi's contribution can be described as a sequence:

1. Identify: recognize an opportunity to collaborate or a contribution that creates value.
2. Agree: settle in advance who takes part, what the action is, its scope and the conditions under which it will be recognized.
3. Carry out: do the contribution as agreed.
4. Certify: produce verifiable evidence that the action was carried out and validated by the participants.
5. Recognize: associate the contribution with a value within the rules of the ecosystem.
6. Reward: let that value become a reward, benefit or opportunity, under the agreed conditions.
7. Connect: use the experience and trust created to make new interactions easier.

This sequence creates a loop in which human action produces evidence, evidence builds trust and trust opens new opportunities to collaborate.

Certifying a past action is only part of Vespi's value. The record can also become a reference for future relationships.

A recognized contribution can help other people learn what a participant can do, trust their experience and make new agreements with them.

Interactions then stop being isolated events and can become the building blocks of a network where value accumulates.

## 8. Why start with micro interactions?

Adopting new technology depends on more than its technical capabilities. It depends above all on people understanding what it is for, finding a concrete use and feeling that the effort of taking part makes sense.

Micro interactions offer a natural way in.

Instead of asking someone to first understand what a DAO is, how a smart contract works or what decentralized infrastructure means, Vespi can invite them to take part in a simple action: collaborate, agree on a contribution and receive verifiable recognition.

This experience is a first centimeter of adoption.

We can think of these participants as opportunity gatherers: people who look at their surroundings, spot possible exchanges and decide to take part when they see that the effort will bring a meaningful reward or benefit.

The challenge is to build systems that understand that logic. Offering incentives is not enough; the experience has to be simple, transparent and consistent with the value each participant receives.

Vespi seeks to study empirically that boundary between effort and gratification. How much effort does an interaction take? What makes a person come back? What kind of recognition builds trust? When does a one-time collaboration become a lasting relationship?

The answers will help design better models of decentralized interaction and offer useful lessons to the whole Web3 ecosystem.

## 9. Vespi's steps: growth in stages

Vespi has to grow step by step. Its growth should depend less on adding features and more on showing that people can use the tool to build valuable, lasting relationships.

The roadmap below has four stages.

### Stage one: validate the basic unit of interaction (short term)

The first challenge is to consolidate the Vespi Contributions model: define and test the basic cycle of agreement, execution, certification, recognition and eventual reward.

At this stage it is essential to experiment with a limited set of participants and types of contribution. The goal is to check that people understand the experience, that agreements are clear enough and that the certificate reliably represents what happened.

We will also need criteria for consent, data protection, validation, dispute resolution and transparency in reward rules.

The expected result is a working, understandable experience that shows a micro interaction can become a verifiable unit of contribution.

### Stage two: build communities of interaction (medium term)

Once the basics work, Vespi will need to extend the experience to communities, organizations and collaboration networks.

The challenge will no longer be only to certify individual interactions but to observe how those interactions create new relationships. Areas to explore include learning, services, professional collaboration, local association and knowledge exchange.

At this stage it will be important to find which kinds of communities get the most use out of the model, which recognition mechanisms work best and how different networks can connect without losing their participants' autonomy.

The expected result is to move from a tool that certifies contributions to an infrastructure that helps collaborative networks form.

### Stage three: connect distributed economies (medium and long term)

With a base of validated interactions and active communities, Vespi can move toward connecting different exchange ecosystems.

Certified contributions could become recognizable references across networks, making agreements easier between participants who do not know each other or belong to the same organization.

This stage will make it possible to explore broader mechanisms of recognition and reward, as well as interoperability with other decentralized applications and protocols.

The expected result is a wider circulation of value, where individual contributions find opportunities in different communities and economic spaces.

### Stage four: move toward an autonomous network of relationships (long term)

The last stage is Vespi's strategic horizon: an ecosystem where people can start, certify and recognize contributions with growing autonomy.

Success will mean that Vespi provides the conditions for participants to create new relationships without depending on permanent central coordination, rather than directing every interaction.

At this stage the model will need to take in what its communities learn, along with participatory governance and shared criteria to keep the system trustworthy and sustainable.

The expected result is a network that produces cooperation, recognition and new opportunities for exchange in a decentralized way.

## 10. How to measure Vespi's impact

Vespi should be judged by its ability to change relationships, and its technological growth is only part of that.

Some relevant indicators:

- New relationships: number of effective collaborations between participants who had not interacted before.
- Capacity to cooperate: share of agreements that are completed and certified.
- Verifiable trust: number of contributions backed by evidence and validated under the agreed rules.
- Less friction: reduction in the time and effort needed to start and complete a collaboration.
- Distributed initiative: share of new interactions started directly by participants, without the Vespi team stepping in.
- Economic recognition: volume and frequency of contributions that receive the agreed rewards or benefits.
- Micro sustainability: how the opportunities, benefits and skills participants gain through the network change over time.
- Continuity: number of relationships that last and lead to new collaborations over time.
- Resilience: the ability of communities to keep coordinating and creating value without permanent central intervention.

These indicators will show whether Vespi is doing more than recording activity: whether it is helping build an associative fabric that produces value in a distributed way.

## 11. Conclusion: recovering the value of people

Decentralized economies face a challenge that goes beyond technology: building new kinds of relationships between people.

Decentralization needs infrastructure, protocols and tools. It also needs ways of taking part that are understandable, useful and meaningful, and it needs people to find concrete reasons to collaborate, trust each other and share the value they produce.

Vespi proposes to start at the scale closest to human experience: the micro interaction.

It offers a space where actions can be agreed, recorded, certified and recognized, where a small collaboration can leave evidence, build trust and open new opportunities instead of disappearing once it is done.

In an increasingly automated world, people can find a new source of value in what connects them: their ability to collaborate, learn, help, coordinate and produce results with others.

Vespi wants to make that value visible and provide ways for it to circulate and be rewarded.

Its proposal is to move from invisible relationships to recognized contributions, from isolated interactions to collaboration networks, and from an economy that concentrates recognition to one that distributes it among those who actually produce value.

Decentralized technology reaches its potential when it also decentralizes the opportunities to take part, contribute and be recognized.

Vespi wants to be that first centimeter: an infrastructure for human interaction so that people can meet, collaborate and build more open, associative and sustainable economies.

In the end, a society's most important resource is not only what it can produce with its machines, algorithms or technological systems. It is also what its people can build when they relate to, trust and work with each other.
